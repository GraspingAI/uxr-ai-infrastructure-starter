# Delegation and Ownership

**State:** Current worked example  
**Owner:** Cyberdyne Research  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-21

The question is not simply “Can AI do this?” It is: **What role should AI have here given the evidence, stakes, consequences, and review available?**

| Work | AI role | Human role | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarify a stakeholder ask | Propose candidate decisions, assumptions, risks, and evidence needs | Decide the framing and research question | Researcher |
| Retrieve indexed material | Locate sources and summarize metadata | Confirm source status and scope | Researcher / ResearchOps |
| Organize evidence | Cluster, compare, surface tensions, draft structure | Decide what patterns are warranted | Researcher |
| Draft a readout | Draft and adapt language for an audience | Approve claims, caveats, implications, and recommendations | Named researcher |
| Check claim ↔ source links | Flag missing support or contradiction | Make the final warrant / confidence judgment | Named researcher |
| Make a product decision | No autonomous decision role | Weigh research with product, technical, legal, and business judgment | Accountable product / organizational owner |
| Maintain shared context and workflow files | May help detect stale text or propose edits | Approve changes, supersede old versions, set policy | Research lead / ResearchOps |

## Shared-system rules

When the workflow becomes shared, the team should be able to answer:

- Who owns the context?
- Where do sources live?
- What requires review?
- What may leave the system?
- Who updates the workflow?
- How do we know it is helping?
- What causes a source, workflow, or rule to be retired?

## What is deliberately not delegated

This worked example does not delegate:

- final interpretation of user evidence;
- claim strength;
- confidence;
- product recommendations;
- participant-data policy;
- decisions about whether evidence from a different population can be generalized.
