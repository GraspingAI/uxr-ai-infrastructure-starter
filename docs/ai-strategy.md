# Worked Example: Research AI Strategy

**State:** Current worked example  
**Owner:** Cyberdyne Research  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-21

This is one possible strategy for one small research team and one bounded workflow. It is not a generic company policy.

## Strategy statement

> We use AI to help plan and draft stakeholder-facing research communication when the decision, source set, and evidence boundary are explicit, using approved indexed research material and reusable project context, with researcher review before any finding, implication, or recommendation travels. We do not use AI output or synthetic material as evidence about users, and we do not delegate final claim strength or product decisions. We maintain context, source status, workflow rules, and ownership so the practice can be reused and updated.

## What that means operationally

**Purpose**  
Reduce repeated setup and drafting work around research planning, synthesis support, and stakeholder readouts.

**Conditions**  
Use the workflow only when the decision, audience, in-scope sources, source status, and review point are clear.

**Evidence / context**  
Use `context/project-context.md`, `sources/source-index.md`, and the approved Cyberdyne corpus. Do not treat every retrievable file as equally valid evidence.

**AI role**  
Draft, reorganize, compare, retrieve, and surface candidate patterns or tensions.

**Human role**  
Frame the decision, interpret evidence, set claim strength, resolve uncertainty, approve findings, and own recommendations.

**Review**  
A named researcher reviews consequential claims against sources before the output enters a stakeholder or decision context.

**Boundary**  
AI output and synthetic material are not user evidence. Product context is not user evidence. Adjacent research stays visibly scoped.

**Infrastructure**  
Maintain project context, source status, workflow rules, evidence boundaries, decision history, and ownership.

## What this kit deliberately leaves out

- automated stakeholder self-service over the whole repository;
- model-specific instructions or vendor-specific architecture;
- automated product recommendations;
- ingestion of confidential participant data;
- a function-wide rollout plan beyond the first pilot.

Leaving these out is intentional. The strategy is broader than the first implementation move, but the infrastructure should grow only when the workflow earns it.
