## Scope

This fictional project explores how operations teams understand and respond to automated task recommendations in an internal workflow product.

The immediate research question is:

> Where do people accept, question, or override automated recommendations, and what information do they need to make that judgment confidently?

## Intended use

Use this context to orient recurring research-planning, analysis, and reporting tasks for this project. It is not a substitute for the source material or for researcher judgment.

## Stable background

- Participants are experienced operations professionals.
- The product surfaces automated recommendations but does not make irreversible decisions on the user's behalf.
- The research team is interested in confidence, interpretability, correction, and escalation behavior.
- Findings should distinguish observed participant behavior from researcher inference and stakeholder assumptions.

## Human role

The researcher owns:

- interpretation of evidence;
- decisions about claim strength;
- contradictory or ambiguous evidence;
- whether AI-generated material is suitable to advance into reviewed output;
- final recommendations and limitations.

## Source rules

- Important claims should be traceable to the source index and underlying approved evidence.
- AI-generated summaries are working material, not source evidence.
- If sources conflict, preserve the disagreement rather than silently reconciling it.
- Missing evidence should remain visible as a gap.

## Privacy boundary

This public example contains only synthetic material. Do not add participant identifiers, raw real-world notes, confidential employer information, credentials, or proprietary research material.

## Known limits

- This context file does not contain participant-level evidence.
- It does not establish whether a finding is current; use the source index and reviewed outputs for that.
- It should remain short enough to review before reuse.
